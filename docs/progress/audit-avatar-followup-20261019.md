## Primary research read before changes

- Unity 6.3 separates the Humanoid body pose from the GameObject's projected root motion. Baking rotation into the pose can leave the actor root unchanged while the animated body faces elsewhere. The Original and Body Orientation import choices have different meanings; an observed reversed pose therefore needs a native clip measurement, not a controller-yaw workaround. [How Root Motion works](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.3/Documentation/Manual/RootMotion.html).
- Unity's Humanoid retargeting uses each model's configured Avatar and recommends an actor root above its animated model. CT Life follows that hierarchy; the logical movement root and the visual pose should be measured separately. [Retarget Humanoid animations](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.3/Documentation/Manual/Retargeting.html).
- `Animator.GetBoneTransform` supplies mapped Humanoid transforms for measuring the actual hips/seat alignment. [GetBoneTransform](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.3/Documentation/ScriptReference/Animator.GetBoneTransform.html).
- Animator culling can stop animation evaluation or transform updates when renderers are invisible. Explicit Animator evaluation is also distinct from the normal game loop. Traffic's pooled, temporarily inactive drivers therefore need checks before and after their next visible animation update. [AnimatorCullingMode](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.3/Documentation/ScriptReference/AnimatorCullingMode.html), [Animator.Update](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.3/Documentation/ScriptReference/Animator.Update.html).

## On-foot camera feedback follow-up

The subsequent Seeker walk check reported a curved route while holding backward. Source inspection confirmed a closed loop: `LocalPlayerController.UpdateDirectMovement` maps the held screen axis through current `CameraRig.yaw` and turns the actor toward it; `CameraRig.LateUpdate` then recenters that yaw toward the actor. The 1.2-second delay is measured since the last explicit camera orbit, not stick-down. `SnapToTarget` sets the timestamp to -10, so a fresh spawn permits recenter as soon as travel exceeds 0.5 m/s. `MovementControls` retains a captured arrow’s direction and refreshes it every frame; it does not issue a camera orbit.

Unity 6.3’s lifecycle documentation places LateUpdate after Update, matching this source path. Unity’s Third Person Follow documentation also distinguishes following the character’s rotation from using a tracking target that can rotate independently. Those references support separating look rotation from the actor’s response to camera-relative movement; no camera package or dependency was added. [Unity LateUpdate](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.3/Documentation/ScriptReference/MonoBehaviour.LateUpdate.html), [Third Person Follow: controlling the camera](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.cinemachine@3.1/manual/CinemachineThirdPersonFollow.html#controlling-the-camera).

The dedicated `OnFootCameraTests` fixture uses real `LocalPlayerController`, `CharacterBody`/CharacterController, and `CameraRig` on an isolated flat collider. A private `FollowOnFoot(float dt)` extraction makes the existing camera step deterministic in EditMode. Tests step actual movement and camera behavior together; they do not substitute a copy of the recenter formula. Native baseline `onfoot-camera-before.xml` recorded 10 cases: 8 failed, 2 passed. Held down/left/right at walk and jog changed camera yaw within 4–11 ticks; the first recorded release-braking swing was 2.392°. Manual orbit also drifted.
